Wow, my first blog! A little bit about myself, for the past eight years
I've been a Quantitative (Quant) Developer. What's a Quant Developer you ask, well its
someone who codes and develops pricing models and algorithms in the financial 
industry. As you might suspect, it's not like it was in years past, things
have been getting worse in finance ...

[![img](http://img.youtube.com/vi/wYlptbR0Dkw/0.jpg)](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wYlptbR0Dkw){:height="180px" width="180px" border="10"}

While I was a Quant I've seen the financial industry blow up in a spectacular way and slowly decay. In this time period, I've seen the exotics and
structured derivatives industry significantly go away. Just like the above movie "Margin Call", banks have exited the derivative business and focused 
on more established services such as corporate finance and non derivative asset management. BTW, one of the best articles I've seen on the why's for 
the 2007 blowup is [Recipe for Disaster: The Formula That Killed Wall Street](https://www.wired.com/2009/02/wp-quant/) and the credit industries focus on 
a single paradigm. Hindsight of course is always 20/20, but when everyone depends on a single way of viewing the world for risk and profit, we 
should expect dire consequences. Lol ... I digress, so let's move to 2016 and the premise of my new blog. It's very evident that there is a new force that is 
"eating up the world", bringing both immense insight and productivity: **machine intelligence**. 

Shivon Zilis, was one of the first reporters to come up with this term "machine intelligence". Machine intelligence is a unifying term for what others call machine learning and 
artificial intelligence. Both of her articles [machine intelligence](https://medium.com/@shivon/the-current-state-of-machine-intelligence-f76c20db2fe1#.maunnvt97) 
and [machine intelligence 2.0](https://www.oreilly.com/ideas/the-current-state-of-machine-intelligence-2-0) are great 
on the current space and the ecosystem of companies that are growing in quantum leaps and bounds. As I read both of these articles one question came to 
mind: *if we are at the start of a revolution in machine intelligence, which is very much model based, what can we learn from the past so we don't
make the same mistakes?* Some light on this comes from a very interesting report on the 2007 credit crisis by Philippe 
Jorion [Risk Management Lessons from the Credit Crisis](http://merage.uci.edu/~jorion/papers/RiskMgtCreditCrisis.pdf). In the report it showed a big difference in the way 
winning and losing firms viewed the management of these models which were used to price and understand their risk. 

Losing firms had risk management practices that had:

* organizations that were hierarchical

* no discussions

* relied on third parties for evaluations

* no testing of models and parameters

Winning firms had risk management practices that had:

* organizations that were cooperative

* shared information across the firm

* developed in-house expertise

* significant testing of their models and parameters

Winning firms had a test driven approach to their model development and execution. Models were not blindly trusted and the firm acted as a learning 
environment to improve and understand the limitations of these models. To me this seems very applicable to this current revolution in machine intelligence. We 
can see in the not too distant future we will have a highly connected 
world of drones, [IOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_things) and machine based 
recommendations, in all phases of our lives. This world will be highly dependent on **production machine intelligence models**, to deliver these services.
In some ways, these production models, could suffer from the same issues that we saw in the credit crisis of 2007. Its therefore extremely important to not blindly
use these models, but instead understand their limitations and assumptions in a **test driven** way. In this blog, **test driven intelligence**, I am going to 
write about this new model revolution from the lens of a test driven approach and also showcase the amazing research that is being done by this community.
I'm very excited about writing this blog and it should be a lot of fun!!
